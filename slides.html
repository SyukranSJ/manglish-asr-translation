Background & Motivation

The Gap

  • Modern ASR achieves near-human performance on clean, formal English
  • Real conversations are informal, accented, multilingual
  • Models trained on formal data degrade significantly in the wild

Manglish

  • Dominant spoken register among educated Malaysians
  • Mixes English + Malay with particles: lah, ah, kan, wah
  • Code-switching mid-sentence, non-standard grammar
  • Not in any public ASR or MT dataset
Commercial speech systems are poorly equipped to handle Manglish — yet it is the everyday language of millions of Malaysians.

Problem Statement & Objectives

Two Core Challenges

1. ASR accuracy
Models can't reliably transcribe Malay words, discourse particles, or code-switched utterances → high WER
2. Cascading errors
ASR errors corrupt MT input → translation quality collapses

Objectives

  • Build a cascaded ASR → MT pipeline for English–Malay
  • Evaluate 4 ASR configurations on Manglish speech
  • Investigate wav2vec fine-tuning under low-resource constraints
  • Quantify the cascading error amplification effect
  • Analyse failure modes qualitatively

Key ASR Findings

Why Whisper wins

  • Multilingual pretraining → knows Malay phonology
  • Trained on internet audio → robust to informal speech
  • Seq2Seq decoder uses context; CTC makes independent frame decisions
"you boleh tolong explain this part tak"

Whisper: "you can help me explain this part, though."
wav2vec: "you bulito loge splendi spite da"

Why KenLM backfired

  • English LM assigns near-zero probability to Malay words
  • Decoder loses word boundaries → merges tokens into single strings
Reference: "i think lah this one better we do tomorrow"
LM output: "itingladisone beteittomoro"
LM integration requires a Manglish-aware LM — a generic English LM actively harms performance.

The Cascading Error Effect

Whisper loses 17.14 BLEU points vs upper bound (64% drop).
wav2vec pipelines (~2.3) are effectively unusable.
WER–BLEU decoupling: Fine-tuning improved WER 0.724→0.700 but BLEU barely changed (2.35→2.28). wav2vec error rate still too high for coherent MT input.

Qualitative Error Analysis

ASR Failure Modes

  • Discourse particles — lah, kan, wah absent from training data; Whisper maps "lah" → "laugh"
  • Code-switched Malay words — wav2vec produces random phonetic sequences; Whisper normalises to English
  • Predominantly Malay utterances — all models fail; fine-tuning makes no difference

MT Error Propagation

  • Hallucination — plausible Malay with no connection to source meaning
  • Literal nonsense — "laugh" → "ketawa" instead of intended "can do"
  • Partial recovery — only Whisper produces enough correct tokens for usable translation
Even with perfect ASR, BLEU upper-bound is 26.78 (below 40) — the MT also struggles with Manglish vocabulary and grammar.

Contributions

  • Novel evaluation dataset — 61 informal English–Malay code-switched utterances with transcriptions + Malay reference translations. First of its kind.
  • Comparative ASR evaluation — 4 configurations under identical Manglish conditions, filling an empirical gap in the literature.
  • WER–BLEU decoupling evidence — improving transcription accuracy does not improve translation when baseline WER is very high.
  • LM integration failure analysis — characterised why standard KenLM fusion fails on code-switched speech.
  • Complete pipeline — reproducible Python codebase (Whisper, wav2vec 2.0, KenLM, NLLB-200) + Gradio web UI.
  • Qualitative error taxonomy — structured classification of Manglish ASR & MT failure patterns.

Limitations & Future Work

Limitations

  • Small dataset (61 samples) — no train/test split
  • Fine-tuning evaluated on training data
  • MT model not fine-tuned for Manglish input
  • Whisper forced English mode — penalises Malay-dominant utterances
  • KenLM hyperparameters not exhaustively tuned

Future Directions

  • Larger, diverse Manglish dataset with proper train/test split
  • Fine-tune Whisper on Manglish — most promising path forward
  • Language-agnostic ASR mode for bilingual utterances
  • Fine-tune NLLB-200 on Manglish input–output pairs
  • Domain-specific KenLM on Malaysian English text
  • End-to-end joint ASR+MT to eliminate cascading errors

Conclusion

Key Findings

  • Whisper (WER 0.415) substantially outperforms wav2vec (0.724) — multilingual pretraining is decisive
  • Fine-tuning yields modest WER gain but no BLEU improvement
  • KenLM degrades ASR to WER 0.916 — vocabulary mismatch
  • Cascading errors: Whisper BLEU drops 64% (26.78 → 9.64); wav2vec ~2.3 (near-zero)
  • Discourse particles, code-switched words, Malay-dominant utterances = core failure modes

Take-home Message

Strong benchmark performance on standard English does not translate to real-world informal multilingual settings.
Improving ASR is the highest-leverage intervention — it unlocks improvements across the entire pipeline.
